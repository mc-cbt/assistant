## Discussion — incomplete profiles and missing Sabre profile IDs

The failure caught yesterday was an **incomplete profile** surfacing only at checkout.

- **Root causes being unpacked:**
  - Clients with **externally managed profiles** (they run Concur expense and don't want us
    pushing data into the GDS) shouldn't be creating users in Andavo at all — but the UX
    still lets them. User creation needs to be **shut off** when that flag is on.
  - One **enrollment flow** doesn't set the **Sabre profile ID** on the traveler's profile
    after creation; repro steps still being pinned down. Workaround is to **retrigger a
    sync**.
  - If no **default rule class** is set when the user is created in Andavo, the **push to
    Faces fails**. Fixed by ensuring a default rule class.
- Users in this state can access Andavo but **cannot book**, and **there's nothing they can
  do about it themselves** — that's the part that needs eliminating. Chad's concern: this
  has been an issue since we started depending on Sabre profile IDs and we'll land back
  here with a future onboarding system.
- **Stopgap agreed**: surface incompleteness **early** — a banner or block at the start of
  booking or at traveler selection — rather than letting someone waste the whole flow. We
  already have the logic to know it will fail.
- **Punted**: a bulk **admin view of incomplete profiles**. It's **expensive to compute**
  (tried roughly three years ago), and an arranger doesn't care about everyone — they need a
  view scoped to **who they arrange for**. Two flows when it happens: the arranger completes
  it if they have the data, or **emails the traveler** to complete their own.
- The traveler-side **onboarding flow** would solve most of this, but it **hasn't been
  rolled out** (mobile isn't on) — so by the release-checklist definition it isn't done and
  can't be relied on.

## Discussion — permissions and policy visibility

- **Don't show booking to users without the booking create permission.** The product has so
  far been role-monolithic — traveler gets all of traveler, admin gets all of admin — and
  **arrangers** break that by holding pieces of admin. Same for travellers who **can't book
  for themselves**.
- There's real demand for this: clients want travellers **forced through an arranger** or a
  **pre-trip approval** process, and some don't want travellers to see booking exists at
  all. Doing it through **permissions** rather than feature switches is the goal.
- Tangent worth keeping: **hiding out-of-policy options erodes trust** and pushes travellers
  to book out of channel. Showing "this hotel is out of policy" is better than showing an
  incomplete picture. Travel managers who say they don't trust their travellers often
  haven't looked at how they manage policy.
