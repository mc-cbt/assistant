# Client-Facing Roles

How the [[Christopherson Business Travel]] / Andavo CSM role is defined relative to the Account Executive and the Operations Advisor, how escalations flow, the small-account playbook, and the first 30 days.

## How we define CSM here

The CSM title means very different things across the TMC industry. At AmexGBT, the equivalent role is heavy on relationship management. At Navan, the Enterprise CSM has commercial responsibilities and runs success plans alongside the AE. At BCD, CSM is often product- or solution-specific.

**Our model is closest to a product-led, technical configuration owner.** You are *not* the contract-and-renewal owner — that's the AE. You are *not* the day-to-day traveler-support layer — that's the Operations Advisor. You are **the person who makes the platform work for the client**: configuration, adoption, optimization, product recommendations.

## The three layers, explicitly

| Role | Owns | Does not own |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Account Executive (AE)** | Renewal, contracting, P&L, multi-stakeholder strategy (IT/HR/Finance/Travel), travel program direction, expansion opportunities. | Day-to-day platform configuration, individual traveler support, technical setup. |
| **Customer Success Manager (CSM)** | Servicing concerns, technology configuration, product recommendations, platform adoption, KPI tracking, training and enablement, identifying optimization opportunities. | Contract negotiation, P&L ownership, traveler-level booking support. |
| **Operations Advisor** | Individual travel requests, traveler support, complex itineraries, after-hours, GDS execution, policy compliance at the booking level. | Strategic program direction, account-level configuration changes, contract terms. |

## How escalations flow

When something goes wrong, escalation flows **upward**. The Operations Advisor handles a transactional issue. If it can't be resolved or it's a **pattern across the program**, it escalates to the CSM. If it's a contract, retention, or relationship-level issue, the CSM brings in the AE. If it touches a beta product or a platform bug, it goes to the **Product Team**.

| Trigger | Owner | When to escalate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A single traveler can't book a flight | Operations Advisor | Resolve at the desk — only escalate if it's a configuration issue. |
| A client's whole team can't access the OBT | **CSM (you)** | Engage immediately. Configuration or integration issue. |
| The client says "we want to rebid the contract" | AE | You hear it first, but the AE owns the response. |
| A bug in Andavo Payments beta | CSM → Product Team | Document, then escalate. |
| Repeated reporting requests outside what's possible today | CSM | Worth aggregating — feeds into the native Reporting beta pressure. |
| A safety/security incident with a traveler | Operations Advisor + Risk Management workflows | CSM and AE notified; client follows their internal protocol. |

## The small-account playbook (where you'll start)

Smaller accounts are where we're moving toward a more automated, **tech-facilitated** account management approach:

- **Small Business:** 10–50 employees, $100K+ travel spend.
- **Mid-Market:** 50–250 employees, $600K+ travel spend.

A traditional model — heavy quarterly business reviews, custom decks, in-person check-ins — doesn't scale and frankly doesn't always serve the client well at this size. They want a platform that works, fast answers when something breaks, and proactive suggestions when something can be improved.

### Five principles for tech-facilitated CSM

1. **Default to async, default to the platform.** If something can be answered with a Loom video, a help-center link, or a configuration change in Andavo Admin, do that first. Reserve live calls for moments that genuinely need them — kickoffs, escalations, strategic conversations.
2. **Group your accounts into cohorts for repeatable plays.** Instead of one-off custom QBRs, run cohort-based touchpoints — e.g., a quarterly "what's new in Andavo" group webinar, a benchmarking email, a templated program-health check. The same content serves many clients with minimal incremental effort.
3. **Lean on data instead of opinions.** Before reaching out, look at their numbers — adoption rate, online booking rate, top exception reasons, unused ticket value, top travelers. Lead with what you observed; ask them what to do about it. Don't show up empty-handed and ask "so, how's it going?"
4. **Automate the rituals.** Welcome emails, training reminders, beta-product invitations, renewal-prep nudges, post-trip surveys — all can and should be automated. Your job is to design the system, not to send each email.
5. **Be the product feedback loop.** You sit closer to the client than anyone else. When you hear the same complaint or request three times, package it up and send it to Product. The native Reporting product is being built in part because beta clients applied pressure through CSMs.

## Your first 30 days

A recommended sequence, not a strict checklist. Adapt based on what's already in flight when you arrive.

| Week | Focus | Concrete actions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Week 1** | Foundation | Read this guide end-to-end. Spend a half-day with an AE on a live client call. Get full Andavo Admin access. Log into Concur Travel as a test admin. |
| **Week 2** | Product depth | Walk through every live solution in [[Product Portfolio]] with the relevant product manager. Get demo accounts where they exist. Sit in on a beta-product internal review (Payments is the most active right now). |
| **Week 3** | Account shadowing | Shadow your first three accounts with the outgoing CSM or AE. Read the last 6 months of email and ticket history per account. Identify any open commitments. |
| **Week 4** | First independent touchpoints | Send your introduction email to each account. Run your first program-health pull (booking volume, online adoption, exception rate, top travelers). Schedule your first 1:1 with each primary client contact. |

### Two things to do before week 4

1. **Internalize the [[Deprecated Terms]] list.** Using "AirPortal", "VirtualPay", or "SecurityLogic" externally will quietly age you in front of clients.
2. **Identify the top three accounts in your book by complexity, not by spend.** Those are where you'll spend most of your time.
