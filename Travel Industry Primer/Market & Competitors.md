# Market & Competitors

Where [[Christopherson Business Travel]] / Andavo fits in the U.S. corporate travel market, our four differentiators, and the four competitors you'll hear about most.

The U.S. corporate travel market splits roughly into **three camps**. Knowing which camp a competitor lives in tells you almost everything about how they sell, where they win, and where they fall over. Our job — and yours specifically as a [[Client-Facing Roles|CSM]] — is to keep clients on the side of the line where the technology-plus-service combination actually compounds value over time.

## The three camps

| Camp | Who lives here | Their sales pitch | Where they fall over |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Service-heavy legacy** | AmexGBT, BCD, FCM, CWT (now part of GBT) | Global scale, board-level relationships, enterprise programs. | Inflexible tech stacks, hidden fees, slow to adopt new UX. |
| **Tech-first / self-serve** | Navan, Perk, Egencia (next-gen) | Modern UX, integrated expense, AI assistants, free or low fee. | Weak after-hours service, limited reporting, no global SLA, high TCO at scale. |
| **Tech-forward [[Glossary#TMC]] (us)** | Christopherson/Andavo, Direct-ATPI | Platform sophistication of a tech company plus the human support of a traditional TMC. | Smaller global footprint than the legacy giants. Newer brand recognition than the household names. |

## Our four differentiators

Every objection-handling moment in your career here will trace back to one of these four pillars. Memorize them.

1. **Technology + service fusion** — Platform sophistication of a tech company with the human support of a traditional TMC. Self-serve travelers get a modern booking experience. Travelers who need help get a 20-year-experienced advisor on the phone. Clients do not have to choose between the two.
2. **Multi-source trip aggregation** — We consolidate bookings from any source (agent, [[Glossary#OBT]], supplier direct) into a single itinerary. Most TMCs only display what was booked through them.
3. **GDS-agnostic content** — We are not locked into a single [[Glossary#GDS]]. We use [[Glossary#NDC]] channels, multiple GDS sources, and direct connects with best-fare logic. Many competitors are tied to one pipe.
4. **Data ownership** — Your data is your data, not ours. No charges for API access, no ecosystem lock-in. A real differentiator vs. tech-first players who use data leverage to keep you in their walled garden.

## The four competitors you'll hear about most

### Navan — the tech-first benchmark

The modern self-serve platform that mid-market and SMB buyers compare us to most often. **IPO'd October 2025**, now public on NASDAQ as **NAVN**. Their AI assistant **Ava** handles ticket servicing, booking modifications, and expense reconciliation. They sell aggressively above the Travel Manager — straight to CFOs and CTOs.

- **Where they win:** Modern UX, integrated expense (Navan Expense), free for SMB under 300 employees, AI servicing automation, low switching cost via free trials. Very good at attracting clients frustrated with legacy Concur.
- **Where they lose:** After-hours support is poor and largely outsourced. No global SLA. Limited reporting that cannot meet most industry-standard KPIs. Per-trip fees create higher total cost of ownership for high-online-adoption clients (most of North America).
- **CSM angle:** Don't badmouth them — they have a real product. Surface where their model breaks down for that specific client: do they travel internationally, have any after-hours travel, need real reporting, have complex itineraries? If yes to any, the technology-plus-service story is the right framing.

### AmexGBT — the enterprise incumbent

The largest legacy global TMC (NYSE: **GBGT**). Closed the **CWT acquisition in 2025** and runs multiple brands — AmexGBT, CWT, Egencia. Major strategic alliance with SAP Concur called **"Complete"** — a co-developed AI-powered T&E platform launched to first customers in **Q4 2025**. Also exploring a potential **sale to private equity** as of late 2025.

- **Where they win:** Board-level relationships, sheer global scale (140+ countries, 27,000+ employees), American Express card ecosystem leverage, perceived ability to manage Concur Travel better post-alliance, and reputation as the "safe" risk-averse brand choice.
- **Where they lose:** Tech stack inflexibility — clients dislike being locked in. "Too big" perception — clients under $25M air spend get lost. CWT integration distraction. Hidden fees for SME clients push TCO above the quoted price. Egencia has known offline service issues and they recently closed Egencia's M&E unit.

### Perk — the SMB-focused tech challenger

Plays where Navan plays but smaller. SMB-focused, expense-integrated, AI-driven. **Significantly upgraded their events product in early 2026 (9 to 5,000 attendees).** Pricing is more transparent than Navan. Will most often appear in the **smallest accounts** in your book.

### Direct-ATPI — the credible bridge

Direct Travel and ATPI combined in **September 2025** to form a **$6.3B TMC** running on the **Avenir/Spotnana** platform. Positioning as the credible bridge between legacy TMCs and pure-tech platforms — **the same positioning we hold**. **Steve Singh (Concur founder)** is involved, lending real tech-narrative credibility. Their CEO is reportedly involved in deals as small as $5M.

- **Watch for:** The **most direct competitor** for our positioning. More credible global footprint than us, but operating with separate leadership across the two brands, and ATPI carried significant accumulated debt going into the merger. Buyer confusion is a real headwind for them right now.
