# TMC Economics

> How the TMC business actually makes money — and where the margin really lives.
> Part 3 of the New Hire Orientation Packet (TMC Economics 101 Training Guide, May 2026).

A Travel Management Company (TMC) sits between corporate travelers and the suppliers who serve them — airlines, hotels, car rental companies, rail providers, and ground transportation. Our value isn't simply booking trips; it's delivering **policy compliance, duty of care, savings, data, and service** at a cost that makes sense for the client and a margin that makes sense for us.

## 1. Welcome — Why This Matters

This guide gives you the working knowledge you need in your first 30 days:

- How TMCs generate revenue (and where the margin actually lives)
- Why online transactions are the single biggest profit lever in our business
- How we scale efficiently by matching service intensity to account value
- What you can do — in any role — to drive profitable growth

> **Bottom line:** Revenue is what clients pay us. Profit is what's left after we serve them. The gap between those two numbers is determined almost entirely by **how efficiently we operate** — and **online adoption is the lever with the most leverage.**

## 2. How TMCs Make Money

TMC revenue comes from a blend of **fees** and **supplier-side income**. The mix varies by client, region, and program design, but virtually every TMC pulls from the seven streams below.

### 2.1 The Seven Revenue Streams

| Stream | What It Is | Who Pays |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Transaction fees** | A per-booking fee charged when a traveler books air, hotel, car, or rail. Online and offline fees are usually priced differently — **offline costs more**. | Client |
| **Management / program fees** | A monthly or annual fee for account management, program design, reporting, and the overall service relationship. | Client |
| **Supplier commissions** | Commissions paid by hotels, car rental companies, and (in some markets) airlines on completed bookings. | Supplier |
| **Override / incentive income** | Performance-based payments from preferred suppliers when we hit volume or share-shift targets. Often the **highest-margin dollars in the P&L**. | Supplier |
| **Service / exception fees** | Charges for after-hours support, complex itineraries, exchanges, refunds, VIP handling, and out-of-policy approvals. | Client |
| **Consulting & advisory** | Sourcing support, benchmarking, RFPs, sustainability reporting, and program optimization. | Client |
| **Meetings, groups & events** | Group air, attendee management, venue sourcing, and meeting logistics — usually a separate line of business with its own margin profile. | Client + supplier |

### 2.2 The Margin Reality

Not every dollar of revenue is created equal. A useful mental model:

- **High-margin:** Override income, online transaction fees, and consulting work. These scale without proportional cost.
